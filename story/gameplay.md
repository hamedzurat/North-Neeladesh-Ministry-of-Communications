# Gameplay

The player works as a telephone switchboard operator. They connect wires, speak to callers in natural language, and make decisions through the work itself. The game has some of the pressure of *Papers, Please*, but it is not a checkpoint game. It is a job simulator about deciding who gets connected, delayed, overheard, misdirected, or denied a connection.

The switchboard has twelve Subscriber Lines, each connected to a physical place. Different NPCs can call from the same place at different times.

The board has one Tap Bridge. Listening to one call means allowing every other call to remain private.

The operator has quick-call access to the police and emergency medical services. A quick call can save someone, expose someone, send armed authorities into a volatile place, or pull scarce responders away from another emergency.

The directory terminal lets the operator look up NPC information, check a caller's story, find relationships, and determine whether someone belongs at the place from which they are calling. Directory records describe what the state believes about a person, which may not be true.

The first complete game will span four work shifts. Calls, connections, delays, surveillance, emergency responses, and conversations will send events down different branches and produce multiple endings.

Side characters should make the ministry and city feel inhabited. Their relationships with the operator must matter outside the struggle for national power.

All old story data, NPCs, place names, and line mappings are disposable. The new story defines what the engine must later support.
