# The Operator

The protagonist is a telephone switchboard operator at the North Neeladesh Ministry of Communications. He is about twenty-four years old.

His father became popular during the war and was killed by sympathizers soon after it ended. The ministry gave the son a job to support the bereaved family. He dislikes the nepotism that put him behind the switchboard, but he cannot afford to refuse it.

His wife is about to give birth. He loves her like hell. Every political risk is also a wager with her safety, their income, and whether he will be present when their child arrives.

The operator is not a member of the persecuted minority. His neighbor was. A mob killed that neighbor, turning the president's campaign against minorities into a death close enough to remember by name.

He does not mourn the president's grip on power, but his wage and his family's security come from the state the president built. His father's death and the ministry job explain why he begins in the political center. They do not pull him into a family mystery.

At first, the factions do not know his name. They treat him like furniture. His influence comes from occupying a neglected point in the communications system while the country fights over who may speak, who may listen, and whose orders are obeyed.
